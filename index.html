Preliminary eye screening, privacy first

Understand your eye symptoms. Keep your photos on your device.

Answer a short symptom check, add eye photos, and get a structured preliminary summary. Gemini Nano analyzes everything on your device. Cloud Gemini is used only if you review what will be sent and approve it.

Preliminary screening, not a medical diagnosis. AI can be wrong, and a phone photo cannot replace an eye exam. Always see an eye doctor if you are worried. In an emergency, call your local emergency number.

How the screening works

  1. 1Capture

    Guided photos of each eye with automatic checks for sharpness, lighting and glare.

  2. 2Symptom check

    Eleven short questions. Fixed safety rules look for red-flag symptoms before any AI runs.

  3. 3On-device AI

    Gemini Nano in Chrome describes visible features and summarizes your symptoms without sending anything out.

  4. 4Report

    A structured report with urgency, limits, next steps and a summary you can show a doctor.

Hybrid inference means the private path is the default. If Gemini Nano cannot run on your device, the app tells you, shows exactly what a cloud request would contain, and waits for your approval. There is no automatic fallback.

Seek emergency care now

    Go to an emergency department or call your local emergency number. Do not wait for this app.

    Add eye photos

    Photos are optional but make the summary more useful. Add one or both eyes. Each photo is checked on your device before anything else happens.

    How to take a good eye photo
    • Hold the camera about 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 in) from the eye, with the eye wide open and looking at the lens.
    • Use soft daylight or an even room light. Avoid the flash and bright reflections.
    • Take off glasses. Keep the whole eye and both eyelids in the frame.
    • Hold still until the picture is sharp. Retake if the check below says so.

            Eye care and finding the right doctor
            
    
            
      
            Everyday eye care
              
        
            • 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes look at something 20 feet (6 m) away for 20 seconds.
              • 
        
            • Blink often and keep screens slightly below eye level.
              • 
        
            • Wear UV-blocking sunglasses outdoors.
              • 
        
            • Do not rub your eyes. Wash your hands before touching your eyes or contact lenses.
              • 
        
            • Get regular eye exams, every year if you have diabetes.
            
        
            
      
            Which doctor?
              
        
            • Optometrist: vision tests, glasses and routine checks.
              • 
        
            • Ophthalmologist: eye disease, surgery and urgent problems.
              • 
        
            • Bring your symptom list, medicines and any past eye reports.
              • 
        
            • Sudden vision change, severe pain or an eye injury: go to emergency care.
              • 
        
            • Search your local doctor directory or ask your insurer.
            
    
            
  
            

  
            
    
            Privacy center
            
    
            
      
            What the app handles
            Eye photos you add, your symptom answers and the optional free text. There are no accounts, no analytics and no advertising trackers.
            
      
            What stays on your device
            Photos and answers are processed in the browser. The on-device screening uses Gemini Nano in Chrome, so nothing is uploaded. Photos are never stored: only a small thumbnail and the report are kept in history.
            
      
            What can go to the cloud, and when
            Only if you press "Send to cloud Gemini" after seeing a preview and ticking the confirmation, or if you press "Plain-language explanation" (text summary only, no photos or symptom text). Cloud requests go through Firebase AI Logic, protected by Firebase App Check.
            
      
            Security checks that do contact Google
            To protect the AI service from abuse, Firebase App Check runs reCAPTCHA Enterprise when the page loads. It verifies the app. It does not receive your photos or answers.

              About, methodology and limitations
              
    
              
      
              What it is
              EyeCheck Assistant is a preliminary screening aid built for the #BuildHybridAI 2026 hackathon on Firebase AI Logic. It organizes symptoms and describes visible features of eye photos so you can decide how quickly to seek care.
              
      
              Methodology
              1) Fixed rules check your answers and text for red-flag symptoms. 2) Photos are checked for sharpness, lighting, glare and size. 3) Gemini Nano (or cloud Gemini after consent) returns a structured JSON description. 4) The app validates it, removes invented numbers, and never lets the AI lower the urgency set by the fixed rules.
              
      
              Limitations
                
        
              • Not a diagnosis, and not clinically validated. No accuracy figures are claimed.
                • 
        
              • A photo of the outside of the eye cannot show the retina or optic nerve. Glaucoma, diabetic retinopathy, macular degeneration and retinal detachment are listed as not assessable.
                • 
        
              • The red-flag rules are conservative but have not been reviewed by a clinician. They can miss cases and can over-warn.
                • 
        
              • Gemini Nano needs desktop Chrome with the Prompt API and a downloaded model. Mobile browsers are not supported for on-device AI.
              
      
              Technology
              Firebase AI Logic (hybrid inference), Gemini Nano via the Chrome Prompt API, Gemini in the cloud, Firebase App Check with reCAPTCHA Enterprise, Vite, plain JavaScript. Open source under Apache-2.0.
